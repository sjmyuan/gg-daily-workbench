# Example: Making Team Documents Concise

**Scenario**: Process-improvement topic — the user's wordy team documents need a way to become concise without losing meaning. Runs through all 8 phases on a non-product topic.

**Applies**: **facilitate-session**
**Phases**: **clarify-idea**, **identify-goals**, **explore-challenges**, **generate-ideas**, **evaluate-ideas**, **challenge-ideas**, **refine-idea**, **compile-actionable-plan**

## Input / Context
User request: "My team's documents — ADRs, solution docs, PR descriptions — are long and wordy. I want to make them concise, not wordy. Not sure where to start."

## Session Walkthrough

### 1. clarify-idea
Facilitator: "What does making documents concise mean to you?"
User: "Say more with fewer words — cut the fluff without losing meaning."
Facilitator: "Who is this for, and what problem does it solve?"
User: "Our engineering team. Wordy docs get skimmed or skipped, so decisions get lost."
Facilitator: "Are you starting fresh, or is there existing work?"
User: "Existing docs — they are written but bloated."
Facilitator: "Any assumption to test — for example, that concise means shorter?"
User: "I assumed shorter, but really every sentence should earn its place."

**Confirmed idea**: A way to make team documents concise — every sentence earns its place while the meaning stays intact.

### 2. identify-goals
Facilitator: "What do you want to achieve — for example, shorter docs, or faster reviews?"
User: "Both. Shorter docs and reviews that actually get read."
Facilitator: "How will you know it succeeded?"
User: "Documents drop by about 40% in length, and reviewers stop skimming."
Facilitator: "Which objective matters most?"
User: "Faster, complete reviews — concise is a means to that end."

**Confirmed goals**: cut document length ~40%; complete reviews, no skimming; a sustainable writing habit.

### 3. explore-challenges
Facilitator: "What challenges do you foresee — for example, writer habits or time pressure?"
User: "Writers are used to long-form, and there is constant time pressure."
Facilitator: "What could cause this to fail?"
User: "If it feels like extra work, nobody adopts it."
Facilitator: "Any constraints, such as tooling or budget?"
User: "No budget for tools — it has to work with a wiki and a repo."

**Confirmed challenges**: long-form writer habit; adoption effort; fear of losing nuance; no tooling budget.

### 4. generate-ideas
Facilitator: "Here are 5–7 diverse ideas, generated by analogy, inversion, combination, and elimination, presented as a mind map:"

```
Concise Documents
├── Analogy — structure
│   ├── BLUF: conclusion first — readers get the answer before the detail
│   ├── One-line takeaway per heading — each section states its point
│   └── Tables over prose — comparisons become scannable
├── Inversion — sentence rules
│   ├── 20-word sentence cap — force one clause per sentence
│   ├── One claim per bullet — no bundled ideas
│   └── Banned-phrase list — remove filler by rule
├── Elimination — editing process
│   ├── "So what?" pass — delete any line that adds nothing
│   ├── Sentence surgery — tighten one sentence at a time
│   └── Before/after pairs — show the standard, not just the rule
└── Combination — feedback loop
    ├── Word-count target — make length visible
    ├── Concise-writing checklist — make the pass repeatable
    └── Review of the reviews — check the checklist is used
```

User: "BLUF with takeaway lines, one claim per bullet, and the 'so what?' pass stand out. Sentence surgery feels too fiddly to start."

**Confirmed candidates**: BLUF structure, one-line takeaways, one-claim bullets, banned-phrase list, "so what?" pass, concise-writing checklist, before/after examples.

### 5. evaluate-ideas
Facilitator: "Scoring the main candidates:"

| Idea | Value | Feasibility | Differentiation | Scope | Impact | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLUF + takeaway lines | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | Readers get the answer first; easy to apply |
| One claim per bullet | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | Removes bundled ideas; common practice |
| Checklist with "so what?" test | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | Makes the pass repeatable; needs drafting |

Facilitator: "By effort vs impact, BLUF, one-claim bullets, and the checklist win. Banned phrases and before/after examples wait; sentence surgery is too fiddly to start."

**Confirmed priorities**: BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, checklist with the "so what?" test.

### 6. challenge-ideas
Facilitator: "Have you considered that hard caps might strip necessary nuance from technical decisions?"
User: "True — treat caps as ceilings, not targets; meaning comes first."
Facilitator: "What if the 'so what?' pass becomes an extra step people skip under time pressure?"
User: "Fold it into the checklist so it is a habit, not a gate."
Facilitator: "Is the checklist essential or a nice extra?"
User: "Essential — it is what makes the habit repeatable."

**Stress-tested ideas**: caps are ceilings, meaning first; "so what?" pass folded into the checklist; checklist is core.

### 7. refine-idea
Facilitator: "What is essential versus nice-to-have?"
User: "Essential: BLUF, one-line takeaways, one-claim bullets, and the checklist with the 'so what?' test. Nice-to-have: banned-phrase list."
Facilitator: "Which remaining risk or gap does the idea still not cover?"
User: "A one-page style guide with one worked example would anchor it."

**Refined idea**: A concise-writing workflow for team documents built on BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, and a checklist whose core step is the "so what?" test, backed by a one-page style guide.

### 8. compile-actionable-plan
The facilitator compiles the plan per **plan-output-format**:

## Plan
A concise-writing workflow for team docs: BLUF structure, one-claim bullets, and a checklist built around the "so what?" test.

## Why this plan
- Reaches G1 and G2 — BLUF and one-claim bullets cut length and make reviews faster.
- Removes C2 — the checklist folds the "so what?" pass into normal review, so it is not extra work.
- Leaves out the banned-phrase list — useful later, not needed to start.

## Goals
- **G1** Cut document length by ~40% — success: a 10-doc sample is ~40% shorter.
- **G2** Speed up reviews so reviewers read, not skim — success: no length or skimming comments in 10 consecutive reviews.
- **G3** Make concise writing a sustainable habit — success: the checklist is used in two consecutive review rounds.

## Decisions
- **D1** Chose BLUF with one-line takeaways — the fastest structural win.
- **D2** Chose one-claim bullets — removes the most common padding.
- **D3** Chose the checklist with the "so what?" test — makes the habit repeatable.
- **D4** Dropped the banned-phrase list — helpful, but not required to start.
- **D5** Added one worked example — anchors the one-page style guide.
- **D6** Open: whether the 20-word cap is a ceiling or a target.

## Risks
- **C1** Long-form writer habit — impact: High — mitigation: one-page style guide and checklist — serves: G1.
- **C2** Adoption effort under time pressure — impact: High — mitigation: fold the checklist into review — serves: G3.
- **C3** Fear of losing nuance — impact: Medium — mitigation: caps are ceilings; meaning comes first — serves: G1.
- **C4** No tooling budget — impact: Low — mitigation: manual checklist and review pairs — serves: G3.

## Next steps
1. **S1** Write the one-page style guide → produces `style-guide.md` — done when BLUF and bullet rules fit one page — serves: G1, C1.
2. **S2** Draft the checklist with the "so what?" test → produces `checklist.md` — done when every item is a yes/no check — serves: G3, C2.
3. **S3** Rewrite one existing doc as the worked example → produces a before/after pair — done when the rewrite is ~40% shorter — serves: G1, G3.
4. **S4** Pilot on two new docs and measure length → produces a length comparison — done when both docs are measured against baseline — serves: G1.
5. **S5** Review adoption and adjust the checklist → produces a revised checklist — done when reviewers report no added effort — serves: G3, C2.

Facilitator: "Here is the plan — it passes the four quality checks. Want to amend any section before we lock it?"
